1. Sensory Anchoring (Appealing to Senses)
You cannot touch a digital product, so you must create "sensory cues" in your design:

Visual Consistency: Use a strict palette (e.g., one primary color, one accent). This creates a "premium" feel. A cohesive, clean site feels "safer" to a buyer than a cluttered one.

Typography: Serif fonts signal trust and tradition; sans-serif signals modern, efficient, and tech-forward. Choose one and stick to it everywhere.

The "Unboxing" Digital Experience: Since you are dropshipping, you control the website unboxing. Use high-quality imagery, clear "added to cart" animations, and reassuring trust badges during checkout.

2. Psychological Sales Triggers
Social Proof: Humans are tribal. Use reviews, "best seller" tags, or customer success stories. If a user sees others have bought it, their "scam radar" turns off.

FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out): Use low-inventory alerts or limited-time pricing to bridge the gap between "browsing" and "buying."

The "Why" Story: People don't buy features; they buy transformations. Tell the story of the problem your product solves or the transformation it offers, rather than just listing technical specs.

Color Psychology: Use Blue for trust/reliability (ideal for electronics), or Black for luxury/sophistication.

Transparent Policies: Clearly display your shipping and return policies. Buyers fear being "scammed"; radical transparency about where the product comes from and how it gets to them is a competitive advantage.

Humanize the Brand: Use "About Us" pages to show the human behind the screen. An authentic voice helps customers feel connected to a person rather than a faceless corporation.

Pathing: Always provide a clear "next step" (e.g., "Add to Cart," "View Shipping Details"). Never leave a user wondering what to do next.

Selling online to the entire U.S. is a major milestone for a business, but it comes with a shift in responsibility regarding sales tax. Since the 2018 South Dakota v. Wayfair Supreme Court decision, you are no longer just responsible for taxes in Louisiana; you may become responsible for collecting and remitting sales tax in any state where you meet "economic nexus" thresholds.  
Thomson Reuters

1. Understanding "Economic Nexus"
You establish "nexus" (a legal obligation to collect tax) in a state in two primary ways:  
Stripe

Physical Presence: Having an office, warehouse, employees, or inventory stored in that state (including inventory held by third-party fulfillment centers).  
Stripe

Economic Nexus: Exceeding a specific financial threshold of sales or transactions within a state.  
Stripe

Common Thresholds: Many states set this at $100,000 in gross sales or 200 separate transactions within a calendar year.  
taxcloud.com

Variations: Every state sets its own rules. Some states, like California, have higher thresholds (e.g., $500,000), while others may count only taxable sales versus gross sales.  
Sales Tax Institute
