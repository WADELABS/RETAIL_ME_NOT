# Pricing Intelligence

## 1. Domain Definition

The Pricing Intelligence domain is one of the most critical components of the ECOS, directly serving the "Profit First" mission. Its sole responsibility is to **recommend a price** and a **listing decision** for a given product from a specific supplier at a specific point in time.

- **Bounded Context:** This domain is a consumer of data from many other domains (Supplier Intelligence, Inventory, Marketing, Risk, Finance) and a producer of recommendations. It does not *set* the final price; it recommends a price to the central Decision Engine. The Decision Engine makes the final call on whether to publish a product at the recommended price. This separation ensures that pricing decisions are made with a holistic view of the business, not just a pricing-centric one.
- **Core Services:**
    - **Pricing Engine Service:** The core service that runs the multi-factor pricing algorithm.
    - **Policy Management Service:** Provides an interface for the Pricing Department to manage the complex rules and policies that govern the engine.
    - **Competitor Data Service:** Ingests and normalizes competitor pricing data from various feeds.

## 2. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

- **Gross Margin / Net Margin / Contribution Margin:** The primary measures of profitability.
- **Margin Leakage:** The difference between the expected margin of a recommended price and the actual margin achieved after a sale.
- **Recommendation Accuracy:** The percentage of pricing recommendations that are approved by the Decision Engine.
- **Algorithm Performance:** p99 latency for generating a price recommendation.

## 3. Data Model

The data model is designed to support complex policy management and provide full explainability for every recommendation.

### Core Tables

- **`pricing_policies`**: Stores the configurable rules for the pricing engine. This allows for dynamic strategies without code changes.
  - `policy_id` (PK), `scope_type` (GLOBAL, CATEGORY, BRAND, SKU), `scope_id`, `rules` (JSONB).
  - **Example `rules` JSONB:**
    ```json
    {
      "minimum_contribution_cents": 7500,
      "target_margin_bps": 1500,
      "reserves": {
        "fraud_bps": 200,
        "return_bps": 500,
        "warranty_bps": 300
      },
      "competitor_strategy": {
        "undercut_cents": 100,
        "premium_tolerance_bps": 250
      },
      "inventory_age_penalty": [
        { "days": 90, "penalty_bps": 100 },
        { "days": 180, "penalty_bps": 300 }
      ]
    }
    ```
- **`competitor_price_observations`**: Time-series data of competitor prices.
  - `observation_id` (PK), `sku`, `competitor_name`, `observed_price_cents`, `source`, `trust_score`, `observed_at`.
- **`pricing_recommendations`**: An immutable log of every recommendation generated by the engine. This is the key to explainability.
  - `recommendation_id` (PK), `sku`, `supplier_id`, `recommended_price_cents`, `calculated_floor_cents`, `expected_contribution_cents`, `expected_margin_bps`, `decision` (RECOMMEND_LIST, RECOMMEND_SUPPRESS), `reason_code`, `inputs_snapshot` (JSONB, a snapshot of all data used), `created_at`.

## 4. The Pricing Algorithm: A Multi-Factor Model

The engine evaluates a SKU/supplier combination by calculating a `calculated_floor_cents` and a `desired_price_cents`, then recommending the higher of the two.

**1. Calculate `calculated_floor_cents`:** This is the absolute minimum price to meet the profit policy.
   `Floor = (Base Costs + Flat Fees) / (1 - Sum of Variable Rates)`
   - **Base Costs:**
     - `supplier_offers.wholesale_cost_cents`
     - `supplier_offers.dropship_fee_cents`
     - `supplier_offers.shipping_cost_cents`
     - `pricing_policies.rules.minimum_contribution_cents`
   - **Flat Fees:**
     - `payments.flat_fee_cents`
   - **Variable Rates (as BPS):**
     - `payments.variable_rate_bps`
     - `pricing_policies.rules.reserves.fraud_bps`
     - `pricing_policies.rules.reserves.return_bps`
     - `pricing_policies.rules.reserves.warranty_bps`
     - `marketing.projected_ad_cost_bps` (from Marketing domain)

**2. Calculate `desired_price_cents`:** This is the market-aware price.
   - It starts with the robust competitor price from `competitor_price_observations`.
   - It applies the `undercut_cents` or `premium_tolerance_bps` from the policy.
   - It is adjusted based on other factors like `inventory_age_penalty`, `demand_trend` (from Analytics), and `customer_lifetime_value` (from Customer domain).

**3. Make Recommendation:**
   - `recommended_price_cents = MAX(calculated_floor_cents, desired_price_cents)`
   - If `recommended_price_cents` violates a hard constraint (e.g., `map_policies.price`), the engine sets `decision = RECOMMEND_SUPPRESS`.
   - Otherwise, `decision = RECOMMEND_LIST`.
   - The entire calculation, its inputs, and the final recommendation are saved to the `pricing_recommendations` table.

## 5. API Contracts (Conceptual)

- `POST /v1/recommendations`: The primary endpoint. Takes a SKU and supplier ID, returns a full pricing recommendation. This is used by the Decision Engine.
- `GET /v1/policies`: List all pricing policies.
- `POST /v1/policies`: Create a new pricing policy.
- `PUT /v1/policies/{id}`: Update an existing policy.

## 6. Key Events

### Consumed
- `supplier.offer.updated`
- `inventory.stock-level.changed`
- `marketing.demand-score.updated`
- `risk.product-assessment.completed`

### Published
- **`pricing.recommendation.created`**: This is the domain's most important event. It signals that a new, actionable pricing suggestion is available for the Decision Engine.
  - **Payload**: The full record from the `pricing_recommendations` table, including the `recommendation_id`, `sku`, `supplier_id`, `recommended_price_cents`, and `decision`.
